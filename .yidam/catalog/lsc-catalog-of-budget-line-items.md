# Catalog of Budget Line Items — what each appropriation line was given

**Source.** Ohio Legislative Service Commission, *Catalog of Budget Line Items*, education volume
(`EDU — Department of Education and Workforce`, formerly `Department of Education`).
**Type.** Primary source, published by the legislature's own fiscal agency.
**Location.**
`https://www.lsc.ohio.gov/assets/organizations/legislative-service-commission/files/{year}-catalog-of-budget-line-items-edu.pdf`,
indexed at `/budget/catalog-of-budget-line-items`. 200 KB–750 KB per edition.

**Editions held: eighteen.** 2006, and 2008 through 2025, less 2012. There is no 2007 edition — a
genuine 404. **There is no 2012 edition either**, which is less obvious: that URL resolves and
returns a PDF, and it is the 2011 document byte for byte. See *One document, two names* below.

**What one entry contains.** Per appropriation line item: the fund, the six-digit line number, the
name, six fiscal years of money with each year labelled, the authorising act, and a prose statement
of what the line pays for.

## Why this is the source the appropriation series needed

**It carries the enacted appropriation and the actual expenditure in adjacent labelled columns.**
That is the defect that reverted the greenbook workbook attempt: there, the variant carrying actuals
*superseded* the enacted column and stated the enacted figure nowhere, so a whole biennium produced
zero appropriation rows without failing. Here the two claims sit side by side, each under its own
heading.

**It reaches both bienniums the greenbook route cannot.** FY2006-07, whose greenbook has no
line-item table at all, is in the 2006 edition. FY2012-13, whose two workbook variants LSC serves as
one file, is in the 2011 edition.

**It names the act.** A `Legal Basis` line gives the sections and act authorising the line this
biennium *and* the act that originally established it — `Sections 265.10, 265.210 … of H.B. 166 of
the 133rd G.A. (originally established by H.B. 66 of the 126th G.A.)`. All 2,008 extracted line
items carry one.

## The two shapes, and why they are not interchangeable

An edition published at the **start** of a biennium carries four `Actual` columns and **two
`Appropriation`** — the enacted amounts. An edition published a year later carries five `Actual`
and **one `Adj. Approp.`**: the biennium's first year has closed into an actual, and the year
remaining is stated as *adjusted*.

| | Editions |
|---|---|
| Enacted (`Appropriation` ×2) | 2006, 2008, 2009, 2011, 2013, 2015, 2017, 2019, 2021, 2023, 2025 |
| Adjusted (`Adj. Approp.` ×1) | 2010, 2014, 2016, 2018, 2020, 2022, 2024 |

An adjusted appropriation is the enacted figure revised by whatever has happened since. Reading one
as the other puts a mid-course number where the enacted one belongs — a plausible figure, in the
right year, from the wrong claim. The extract keeps three kinds and never two.

**So the enacted figure for any biennium is in exactly one edition**, the one published when it was
enacted. That is what makes a continuous series possible: GRF 200550 Foundation Funding has one
enacted appropriation per fiscal year from FY2006 to FY2027, each from a single edition.

## One document, two names

The 2012 URL resolves, returns `application/pdf`, and is the 2011 edition byte for byte. The
decision record that scoped this work counted nineteen editions because it verified existence by
status code and content type; only a digest catches this. It is the same pathology
`the-greenbook-series` found in the workbooks — LSC serving one file under two names — and wiring
both would have double-counted every FY2012 and FY2013 enacted appropriation under two vintages
that are one document.

## What it is not

**Not a record of what one act appropriated.** It is a standing reference restated each edition, so
a figure in the 2019 edition is that line's history *as of 2019*. It complements the greenbooks
rather than replacing them, and the extract keeps `edition` as a column for exactly that reason.

**Not the pre-2002 record.** The earliest edition reaches FY2002. The Foundation Program era,
*DeRolph I* and the equal yield formula still need the session laws.

**Not per-district.** These are appropriation lines, statewide. Nothing here says what a district
received.
