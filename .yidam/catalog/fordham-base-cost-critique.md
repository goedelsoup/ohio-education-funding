# Fordham Institute — Ohio Base Cost Model Commentary

**Source.** Thomas B. Fordham Institute (Ohio), commentary series on the Fair School Funding
Plan: *Ohio's new school funding formula — challenges with the base cost model* and *An
overview of Ohio's new school funding formula, and a look at whether it might stick*.
**Type.** Secondary source — policy analysis and critique.
**Location.** `fordhaminstitute.org/ohio/commentary/`.

**What it contains.** Analysis of the base cost methodology's structural properties, with
figures this corpus uses for context rather than for computation: the pre-FSFP base cost of
$6,020 per pupil in FY2021, the statewide average of $7,352 in FY2023 and $8,240 in FY2025,
the stale-input problem (teacher salaries rose from $62,353 in 2018 to $67,654 in 2022, an
8.5% increase, while the model continued pricing from FY2018), and the small-district effect —
districts under roughly 700 students, about one-sixth of Ohio districts, carry base amounts
above $8,000 per pupil because minimum staffing floors apply regardless of enrollment.

**Why a critical secondary source is catalogued at all.** The small-district and stale-input
observations are not in the department's methodology documentation, which specifies the
formula without evaluating it. They are the kind of claim the corpus needs in order to ask
whether a mechanism does what it says, and they come with a point of view — Fordham is a
school-choice-aligned organization and its critique of the base cost model should be read
knowing that.

**Access constraints.** Freely available.

**Caveat.** Figures here are analysis, not the official record. Where they overlap with
[`dew-sfpr-line-by-line`](dew-sfpr-line-by-line.md) the department figure governs — note that
Fordham's $7,352 for FY2023 and the department's $7,349.22 for FY2022 are different fiscal
years, not a discrepancy, and combining them without checking that would produce a false
sense of a flat series.
